The Anthropometric Data for Interior Designers
Indian Standards

Most heights and sizes used in Indian homes come from Western reference books, measured on different bodies. The result is familiar on site: counters that strain the back, top shelves nobody can reach, sofas too deep to sit back in.

This is the fourth Air Academy manual — a working reference of Indian body measurements, turned into the design numbers you actually need when you draw a kitchen, a wardrobe or a bedroom.

What the book will cover
Body dimensions of Indian men and women — standing, sitting and reaching
Kitchen working heights, wall-unit reach and clearances
Wardrobe, loft and storage zones you can actually use
Seating, dining, bed and work-desk sizes
Bathrooms, stairs and doors — including homes for elders and children
Quick-reference tables you can keep open while drawing
Best for
Designers, draughtsmen, carpenters and students who size furniture every day
Why it helps
Fewer site corrections, and clients who feel the difference
Questions it answers
Why do imported kitchen heights feel wrong to the person who cooks every day?
How high can the top wardrobe shelf go before nobody uses it?
What changes when you design for the client's parents?
